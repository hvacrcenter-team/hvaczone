*(Homeowner audience. Lead with value, link naturally. Check each sub's self-promo rules before posting.)*

**Title:** Your AC "can't keep up" in this heat wave? It probably isn't broken — here's how to tell.

**Body:**

With the heat dome pushing 90s–100s into the Northeast starting Tuesday, a lot of people are about to panic that their AC is failing. As an HVAC contractor, this is the call I get most. Most of the time the system is fine — it's just working against conditions it was never sized to fully overcome.

The key idea: a residential central AC is designed to hold your house about **15–20°F below the outdoor temperature** (that's the ACCA Manual J sizing standard the DOE references). So when it's 98° outside, your system holding 78–80° inside is doing its job — not failing.

The real diagnostic question is whether that gap is **holding**. If indoor stays ~18–20° below outdoor all day, it's working. If the gap is shrinking — indoor creeping up while outdoor holds steady — that points to a real problem.

Before you call a tech, check:
- Replace the air filter (clogged = the #1 cause of lost airflow)
- Clear 2 ft around the outdoor condenser, hose off the fins gently (no pressure washer)
- Close blinds on sun-facing windows
- Skip the oven/dryer in the afternoon
- Set a realistic target (76–78°), not 68° — cranking it colder doesn't cool faster, it just runs nonstop

When it actually IS broken: lukewarm air from the registers, short-cycling on/off, ice on the lines, or indoor temp rising even as outdoor drops.
